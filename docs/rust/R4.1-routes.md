## What forwards

Forwarding is the rule for anything these reads can't say exactly, and a forward's answer is Python's own.

| Case | Why |
|---|---|
| A snapshot that isn't fresh; a field a route needs is absent; a markerless or behind poll whose overlay changes the answer | The live rule (`contracts/live-snapshot.md`) |
| **`GET /fluid/api/response-sets?post=` and `/response-sets/<id>`: always. No Rust route** | Both embed a set's state, which needs `_block_check` and the render engine. **So "response sets" in row 1 is only partly moved.** These two move when the engine's write-side callers do (R7) |
| `/accounting` on a set holding an interpretation, or whose form Python refuses (no such text part, no `post`, no `block`) | An interpretation's view reads the block's fields through the engine, and the refusal is Python's |
| A set the record doesn't hold, or a post the anchors route refuses | The refusal is Python's own shape |
| A reaction another act superseded | Its `replaced_by`/`replaces`. The master holds none; `routes_diff shapes` writes one |
| A record that names no owner | Python's "unknown" wording. The copy names one |
| A cursor or limit `float` and `int` read and these don't; words whose `repr` isn't plain | Python says what they mean |
| A value of a type Python would raise on, or show some other way | Python's answer, its 500 included |
| Any read that fails (busy, out of its scope's time) | Never a 503 of Rust's own while Python can answer |

`_avatar` stays exact by looking: `describe(verify="cached")`, through the window's existing once-per-file-version hash. The first `/api/participants` hashes the 17 pictures, within its read scope; if that outruns the scope, the read forwards.

**How much of each population.** Whole: Conversations, every post through anchors and selection, every request through `needs-you?ids`, every post carrying a request through `needs-you?post`, every stream page, every response set. Not whole:

- **`changes` answers only within 200 rows of the head,** so the whole record can't be asked of one copy. `history` asks it of the record as it stood at 12 moments, evenly by entry, each with its 87 windows. Windows between those moments were not asked.
- **Participant searches and activity searches** are cuts of what the record holds (1- and 3-letter prefixes, words of names), not every string: 30 of each, by a fixed seed.

- **What the shapes copy asks** is a sample of 30 where the master's copy asks everything, because the shapes are few and sit on a small part of the record.
- **What the master doesn't hold** was written by Python's own functions on a copy (`routes_diff shapes`): reactions changed, withdrawn, deleted by another and generically superseded; an edited and a deleted post under anchors; own words and an interpretation; asks reassigned, met and left on a deleted post; renamed Conversation, Topic and Participant; a picture's bytes gone and changed; two designations standing. Python's own refusal of blank own words means that shape isn't there.

**Real producers.** `test_r4_real_live.py` moves `begin_execution` and `agents_open_run` inside Python's process (`tools/r4/live_python.py`) and reads through the real poller: two executions, one placed nowhere, a real open run covering its participant, all ended. `test_r4_routes.py` scripts `/private/live`, the only way to hold a poll in flight: stale, resume after idle, changed boot, lower generation, unknown schema, each field absent, unavailable past 10 s with durable siblings answering and the exact 503 words, the recovery poll, an end landing in flight, a moved record whose overlay changes nothing, and the correcting reread. Markerless and behind-in-each-component are op-level, in `tests/r41_lists.rs`, because no front can be made to show them on demand.

**A fresh poll can lag.** Fresh means up to 2 s old by contract, and polling stops 5 s after the last pulse. So a client that pulses only now and then can see a count up to 2 s behind the execution that changed it, with no forward. `test_r4_real_live.py` first failed on exactly this, and now waits a poll out. It's the contract's own window, not a defect, but it is a real difference from Python.

**The same test can fail on a busy machine.** It asserts that nothing forwards, so a poll that times out fails it: in one run a poll timed out, the snapshot read `Stale: age 5.17s`, and `?activity=1&limit=24` forwarded, as the contract says it should. Three reruns passed. That run shared the machine with this document's load probes, among other work, and what delayed the poll wasn't established.

### The 60 seconds in that table

**They are mostly the harness, on a real weakness of Python's, and not the machine or Rust.** Every figure here is from a copy, on Python alone unless it says so.

- **What 60 s is.** The harness's client gives up at 60 s and records that as the time (60,013-60,030). Its spinners also run for exactly 60 s (`(15 + 3) x 3 + 6`), and the other figures (54.8-59.6 s) are a forward finishing as they stop. With the limit raised to 300 s and front and relay in place, the same 8-and-8 level's forward came back at 69.5 s, a correct 200 of 25,503 bytes, whose first computation took 60.48 s and its retry 2.63 s. So the true figure is "until the spinners stop", and the 60 is a coincidence of two of the harness's numbers.
- **Why it blocks.** A forward of `/api/conversations` after a write is a cold `conversation_summaries`, about 1.3 s idle, reading many rows. `sqlite3` gives up the GIL at each step and has to get it back from a thread that never gives it up willingly. With a longer spin and a 240 s limit, 2 pure-CPU threads held that cold read and a POST past the limit, and 8 did too: nothing finished until the spin ended, 630 s in. With `sys.setswitchinterval` taken from 5 ms to 0.5 ms the same cold read took 13.5 s under 2 threads and 85.8 s under 8, which is what makes this the GIL and not the database.
- **It isn't the machine.** It has 20 logical processors, and Rust's reads through the same front on the same record stay at p95 151-265 ms in those rows, apart from `activity`'s forward tail.
- **It isn't Rust.** A forward is Python's answer, and Python alone is no quicker: those 630 s were without any front. Only forwards land on this, while the rest of each row is Rust answering.
- **What a Python busy with real work does.** Without spinners, 8 threads reading the same routes and a write every 5 s (no front) take 7.1-11.7 s for the first cold read after they start, and 1.2-3.0 s after that. A read that a write lands on takes the whole computation again (`Corpus.settled`), and did so at most twice in these runs.
- **What makes it worse: a cold read longer than the gap between writes.** `settled` tries three times and then takes the per-Conversation reads. With 2 threads spinning, the 0.5 ms switch interval and a write every 5 s, each try took 12.5-14.7 s, a write landed on each, and that fallback took 42.5-44.7 s more: 84.7 and 86.3 s, against 13.5 s with no writes. It was reached only in that condition's two reads. With 8 threads spinning at that interval the read passed the 240 s limit. That is Python's own, and it would meet a forward the same way.

So under the gate's 8 spinning threads, a forward of this route waits as long as those threads run. Whether anything in Mentis holds the GIL that way wasn't examined, so read that level as showing the weakness, not as a forecast. Forwarding adds little to it: a client of Python alone would meet the same wait on every cold read.

**One thing is not explained.** In the 8-and-8 row Python's three direct reads, taken after the spinners had stopped, each hit the 60 s limit. Neither replay shows that: the level alone, with the front, gave 2,455, 1,129 and 26 ms, and on Python alone 1.3 s and then milliseconds. The replays ran that level first, while the original ran it last, after four levels whose abandoned requests Python goes on computing (its log holds 13 `ConnectionAbortedError`s from clients that gave up). That backlog is the likely cause, but it wasn't shown.

## Reconciling with R4.2

R4.2 (`r4-2-views`, read at `b517dc5`, still being corrected; its route table and `overlay.rs` rechecked at `3707e64`) was read and not merged. Integration has to reconcile:

- **Text conflicts, append both:** `crates/mentis-ops/src/lib.rs` (module list), `crates/mentis-server/route-table.default.json`, `handlers/mod.rs` (`ROUTES`), `crates/mentis-server/src/lib.rs` (`HANDLERS`).
- **`handlers/window.rs` and `window/classify.rs`:** both branches change them. Here `ids_named` becomes `pub(super)` and `OWNER` gets `+family`.
- **Route order:** both tables keep `{"id": "api", "path": "/api/*"}` last. R4.2's `/api/conversations/{}` and `/api/conversations/{}/thread/{}` don't match this `/api/conversations`, so no route changes hands.
- **`/fluid/api/decisions`:** R4.2 moves it, so integration can remove it from `_POLLED_PATHS`, which `test_polled_reads_kept.py` then shows. It stays here because R4.1 doesn't own it.
- **`overlay.rs`:** R4.2 adds `execution_bool`; this package doesn't touch that file. No conflict.
- **`mentis-rules` plans:** `LINE_LINKS` and `LINKS_REACHING` change here, in plan only. R4.2 and R5 read through them and get faster; their cost figures taken before this don't describe the merged result.
